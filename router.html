The Router

Which machine-payable endpoint should you call, is it alive right now, and what does it charge? Answered by probing — never by paying.

Why this is not obvious

The x402 Bazaar keeps an entry for 30 days after its last settlement, and the MCP registry lists whatever a publisher declared. So listed and answers are different facts about 24,982 catalogued endpoints, and only one of them was published anywhere.

The trick that makes this cheap: an unpaid request to a paid endpoint returns its own 402, and a 402 states its terms. Liveness and price come back in the same free request. Nobody has to pay to find out what something costs.

Two endpoints

GET /api/liveness?url=… $0.005

Probes one endpoint now: whether it answered, how fast, and the terms it currently quotes — parsed from its own 402, in either x402 version.

curl "https://router.percall.dev/api/liveness?url=https://example.com/paid"

POST /api/route $0.005

Ranked candidates for a task, each probed live, each with the URL to call and what it quotes. Endpoints that did not answer are reported, not hidden — you asked what exists. A query that matches nothing is free.

curl -X POST https://router.percall.dev/api/route \
  -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"q": "unit conversion", "max_price": 0.01}'

History, and why it gets better

Every answer carries that endpoint's record: total probes, how many answered, consecutive failures, and how many of the last 30 observations answered. It is built from live probes rather than a crawl, so it deepens on exactly the endpoints people ask about — the answer improves the more the service is used.

The uptime ratio is withheld below three observations. "Answered 1 of 1" is technically true and practically a lie, and you would compare it against another endpoint's number anyway.

It never pays, and that is structural

This service holds no key that could sign a transfer. It hands you the URL and the terms; you pay the endpoint directly, from your own wallet. Nothing is proxied, so the operator you pay sees you rather than us — your per-caller pricing and rate limits keep working.

The probe sends two headers and nothing else. Your request may carry a payment authorization made out to us, and forwarding request headers to a third party would hand a signed credential to a stranger, so the probe is built from scratch every time. A test forbids any module here from setting an outbound payment header at all.

Paying for it

Both endpoints are $0.005 in USDC on Base over HTTP 402 — no account, no dashboard, no human step. Read the terms without provoking a 402 at /api/x402/info.

Being kind to the endpoints

Probes are shared for 60 seconds, so a popular endpoint is probed once a minute however many callers ask, and the candidate fan-out is capped. These are other people's servers. If you operate one of the catalogued endpoints and want it gone, open an issue on the repo — no justification needed.
